This supplement gives the definitions, model specification and results for D18. The 18-predictor L2 model was selected using December 2023 to December 2024 development data and frozen before first access to the 2025, 2026 and MIMIC-IV validation cohorts. It remained unchanged after validation. Model development, temporal validation and the adapted MIMIC-IV transportability assessment are distinguished from the subsequent exploratory analyses.

Supplementary methods

S1 Data sources and culture events

We used the frozen hospital and MIMIC-IV event cohorts for this study. Hospital data came from the two campuses of The Second Affiliated Hospital of University of South China, treated as one centre. Available hospital records began on 1 December 2023, after replacement of the hospital information system. Study entry identified culture requests through 79 culture catalogue items in the laboratory information system, independently of organism identification, susceptibility results and report status. Screening and mixed-purpose cultures, identity failures, invalid admission links and timing failures were excluded. Within each admission, the first unassigned eligible request defined T0 and opened a fixed 24-hour window. Later requests within that window supplied outcome evidence without changing T0 or extending the window. Patients were assigned to their first study period; events in later periods were excluded before the age filter was applied.

Eligibility required age ≥18 completed years at T0. Hospital age was checked against linked birth dates for all 72,079 events before age restriction, with no disagreement recorded against the frozen age field. Missing age could not be imputed for eligibility. The restriction retained 61,448 hospital events, preserving their membership, T0, period assignments and predictor history. Patients turning 18 during admission could contribute eligible later events, and valid history before that birthday remained available.

All 128,320 requests reconstructed from source-system snapshots were present in the 128,959-request study entry. Of the 639 entry requests not recovered in the reconstruction, 623 lacked an original request date and admission key and were quarantined; the remaining 16 dated differences were not fully explained. Because the snapshots shared a source with study entry, their agreement confirms extraction continuity but cannot verify hospital-wide completeness independently. These are reconciliation differences between extracts of the same source. Table S1 distinguishes request counts from event counts after the age restriction. Clinical characteristics describe the included events.

S2 Outcome evidence and microbiology

Outcome classification used the final fixed version of the outcome rules. Hospital reports with formal final status 3 or 4 underwent evidence assessment. After source-system review, IsNotReport, Reported and CancelFlag were not used as independent exclusion criteria. We checked report versions, patient identity, links between reports, isolates and susceptibility results, and conflicting evidence. A confirmed formal case-level conclusion could support a positive outcome when structured susceptibility results were unavailable. Formal no-growth reports, confirmed normal flora and non-target organisms could support non-positive evidence. An event was positive if any request was positive and non-positive only if every request had sufficient non-positive evidence; all other events were unknown. Missing or unverified reports were never assumed negative. The endpoint measured detection without adjudicating infection.

Enterobacterales were classified as CRE if resistant to an applicable carbapenem (imipenem, meropenem, ertapenem or doripenem), except that imipenem alone did not define CRE for Proteus, Morganella or Providencia. CRPA and strictly defined Acinetobacter baumannii CRAB used imipenem or meropenem. Only reported resistant categories counted as resistance. A target isolate supported non-positive evidence only if at least one applicable carbapenem had been tested, all tested applicable agents were susceptible, and no resistant or conflicting result was present. Intermediate-only, missing, uninterpretable or conflicting results remained undetermined. Preliminary Gram stains did not determine outcomes. MIMIC-IV used reported interpretation categories, with Edwardsiella tarda and Plesiomonas shigelloides included among Enterobacterales.

At both hospital campuses, identification and susceptibility testing used DL-96A and DL-96II systems with DL-96E and DL-96NE panels (Zhuhai DL Biotech, China); mass spectrometry was not used. Imipenem and meropenem were routinely tested; ertapenem and doripenem were not. Resistant or atypical findings could be checked by gradient diffusion, disk diffusion or carbapenemase testing according to the organism. The laboratory routinely updated its susceptibility interpretation criteria in accordance with successive CLSI M100 editions, typically implementing new editions approximately one month after their release. Susceptibility categories were retained as reported at the time of testing, without retrospective reinterpretation using a single CLSI M100 edition. Target organisms in reports described as normal flora were reported separately; mixed growth with a request for repeat sampling was treated as contamination and did not supply non-positive evidence. [AUTHOR TO COMPLETE: date of the final report snapshot used for outcome determination.]

The outcome dictionary and external mapping were fixed for the analyses. Unknown events remained eligible for alert allocation but were excluded from fitting and known-outcome discrimination and calibration. The specimen-specific unknown-outcome summaries in Table S14 do not identify reasons for indeterminate results. The main analysis retains the 24-hour outcome; T0-only analyses appear in Method S11 and Table S18.

S3 Predictors and model development

Table S2 defines all 18 raw predictors. Hospital laboratory categories used each report's reference range; no qualifying result by T0 was coded as missing. Medication and device variables represented records of orders or procedures and could not confirm administration or device presence at T0. Target-organism and target-resistance history required formal evidence in [T0 − 90 days, T0), excluding every request in the current event. No qualifying positive record was coded MISSING because absence could not be confirmed. Valid earlier records remained available even if they preceded age 18.

D18 includes t0_specimen, derived only from all requests tied at T0. The fixed domain is RESP, BLOOD, URINE, OTHER, MULTIPLE and MISSING. Multiple broad groups produce MULTIPLE; any unmapped type produces MISSING. Later requests within the event cannot contribute to this predictor. Mapping uses specimen metadata without outcome labels. Frozen-trace reconstruction is reported in Table S17; historical field revision logs were unavailable.

We fitted an L2-penalised logistic regression to 24,501 known-outcome development events from 14,916 patients, including 1488 positive events. Numerical predictors were median-imputed and standardised within each training fold. Neither numerical predictor was missing in the final fitting data, so no numerical missing-indicator columns were generated. Categorical predictors were one-hot encoded using fixed domains including missing categories. Five outer patient folds supplied development predictions; four inner folds selected C from 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10 by minimum mean log loss. A separate five-fold development search selected C = 0.1 for the final fit. All outer fits also selected C = 0.1. We used the liblinear solver, equal event weights and no class weighting or resampling. D18 has 51 encoded coefficients and an intercept (Supplementary Tables S3 and S4). Every fold model and the final model were newly fitted. All eligible records in the fixed periods were used without a formal a priori sample-size calculation [22]. Patient-cluster intervals describe evaluation precision [23]; the evaluation cohorts contained 767, 649 and 1181 positive events.

The preprocessing transforms numerical inputs as (x − training mean)/training standard deviation after median imputation. One-hot encoding retains every listed level; an absent category is not silently treated as the reference level. The linear predictor is the intercept plus the sum of encoded inputs multiplied by their coefficients. Probability is the inverse logit of this linear predictor. The saved model is authoritative; Table S3 provides its full coefficient set and numerical preprocessing.

L2 was selected after development comparison with random forest and XGBoost using the same predictors and patient folds (Method S16; Tables S27–S31). We considered transparency, ease of audit and reproduction, and uncertainty in additional cumulative coverage. The complete D18 model was fixed before first access to all validation cohorts. Predictor ablations and restricted-training fits were separate exploratory comparisons. Table S15 summarises discrimination among H90-unflagged events and coverage at each budget.

S4 MIMIC-IV transportability assessment

MIMIC-IV version 3.1 used the frozen study-specific cohort and source mappings. We grouped events in fixed 24-hour windows with specimen chart time as T0. Age was anchor_age plus the calendar-year difference from anchor_year under the source de-identification scheme, so it does not give exact original chronological age. Medication proxies combined prescription, order and pharmacy records; device proxies used timed ICU procedure records. Procalcitonin had no mapped source item and was missing by design. Laboratory results had to meet both time-window and storage-time criteria. Microbiology history used earlier available specimens outside the current event. We did not refit or recalibrate predictor parameters, coefficients or preprocessing. The original external outcome mapping, including indeterminate flora comments, was retained for the primary analysis. A separate restricted respiratory-flora hypothesis is reported in Method S19; it does not replace the primary mapping. The MIMIC-IV cohort was first accessed after D18 had been frozen. Differences in the data source, timing proxy and clinical mappings make this an assessment of transportability rather than confirmation of performance.

The T0 specimen input used co-earliest specimen chart times with the same broad-category domain. This retains the source timing proxy and cannot establish equivalence to a live order-entry field.

S5 Alert allocation and coverage endpoints

The primary strategy endpoint, the 1%, 2% and 5% extra alert budgets, and the comparator rules were specified after reviewing the 2023–2024 development results and before first access to the 2025 and 2026 hospital validation cohorts. H90 flagged every event with a qualifying target-resistance report in the preceding 90 days. It simulated an automated history summary; during the study period, the hospital had no automated resistance-history alert and clinicians consulted reports individually. All strategies retained every H90 alert. The remaining N events, including unknown outcomes, received an extra quota of ceil(N × p), where p = 1%, 2% or 5%. The model ranked predicted probabilities and broke ties with a fixed event-identifier hash. ICU priority allocated the same quota to ICU events first, then to other events, choosing uniformly without replacement within each stratum. Hypergeometric probabilities gave its expected number of distinct patients covered, which may be fractional. ICU+LOS prioritised ICU events, then decreasing length of stay before T0, using the same fixed tie rule. These budgets specified alert capacity, not risk thresholds. Allocation used the complete evaluation period and did not simulate daily queues.

Cumulative additional coverage counted distinct patients with at least one supplement-alerted positive event and no H90-flagged positive event anywhere in the same evaluation period. A later H90-positive event therefore excluded a patient from this count. Burden included all extra alerts, including repeats and unknown outcomes. Alerts per additional patient equalled extra alerts divided by cumulative additional patients; a zero denominator gave an infinite ratio. For reference, H90 burden was H90 alerts divided by H90-covered positive patients. These ratios describe alert volume without measuring review time, clinical benefit or diagnostic tests per patient. Known-outcome PPV excluded unknown alerts from its denominator; confirmed yield used all alerts.

S6 Uncertainty and probability performance

The original analyses used 1000 paired patient-cluster bootstrap replicates [28]. Patients were resampled with all their events; eligible pools, quotas, rankings and patient counts were recomputed with fixed scores. The later analyses in Methods S22–S24 used 5000 replicates. Percentile 95% intervals are pointwise and unadjusted for multiplicity, and exclude model fitting, tuning and algorithm-selection uncertainty. Draws were shared within paired comparisons; seeds could differ between runs. The original primary intervals are retained, so identical baseline estimates can have slightly different limits across analyses. Calibration regressions were refitted in each replicate: CITL used an offset logit with slope fixed at 1; the joint intercept and slope were fitted separately. Decile intervals used the original score bins. Model scores were not recalibrated.

Table S6 reports discrimination, probability loss and calibration, including CITL, joint intercept, slope, observed/expected ratio and Brier skill. All 1000 calibration replicates were valid in each population and cohort. Fixed-bin intervals are pointwise rather than simultaneous bands. Figure 3 shows decile point estimates and intervals for the calibration diagnostics.

S7 First known positive capture

For each patient with a known-positive event, we identified the event with the earliest T0 in the evaluation cohort. A strategy captured the patient only if it alerted that event. Selection still used all candidate events, including unknown outcomes, without restricting the pool to first or positive events. The supplement count excluded events already flagged by H90 at that T0, and combined capture added the H90 first-capture baseline. A later H90 alert did not erase an earlier first capture. This endpoint differs from cumulative additional coverage and from restricting evaluation to each patient's first culture event. In scenarios assigning unknown outcomes positive, we recomputed the earliest positive event, which could then be an originally unknown event. The first known-positive event need not be the first true positive event, and this analysis did not measure clinical lead time. Unknown-outcome comparisons also assessed this endpoint.

S8 Unknown outcome sensitivity and identification bounds

Unknown outcomes. The observed-label analysis was compared with two deterministic scenarios: every unknown event assigned non-positive and every unknown event assigned positive. Each scenario recomputed positive-patient baselines, cumulative additional coverage and first positive events. Scores, H90 flags and alert selection remained fixed to the original evidence; hypothetical labels were not written back into subsequent history. All scenarios were evaluated against both simple comparators at all three budgets. The all-positive and all-non-positive scenarios are not necessarily worst-case bounds.

Sharp identification bounds. Each unknown event could take either binary label, with one consistent label vector applied to both strategies. For cumulative coverage, feasible assignments were evaluated at the patient level after accounting for H90-positive coverage and supplement selection; extrema were then combined across patients. For first capture, candidate earliest-positive events included known positives and feasible earlier unknown positives, with later alerts receiving no credit. The resulting minimum and maximum differences describe the fixed observed data and allocation rules, not sampling confidence intervals. Where reported, the tipping count is the minimum number of unknown events needing adverse positive assignments, from an all-unknown-non-positive baseline, to make the cumulative difference non-positive. A nonattainable tipping value is distinct from zero.

Table S13 reports bounds against ICU+LOS and ICU priority. Strictly positive bounds rule out a non-positive difference only for allowed unknown-label assignments within the current fixed sample, with known labels, scores, H90 flags, quotas and selection rules unchanged. They do not account for sampling, misclassified known outcomes, changed predictor values or future distribution shift. The uniform scenarios in Tables S11 and S12 have bootstrap intervals; the sharp bounds do not.

S9 Analysis sequence and reproducibility

The study proceeded through comparison of L2 logistic regression, random forest and XGBoost on the December 2023–December 2024 development data; retention of L2; finalisation of the complete D18 model; and first access to the validation cohorts. D18’s 18 predictors, preprocessing, coefficients and penalty parameter were fixed before the 2025, 2026 and MIMIC-IV cohorts were first accessed and were not changed after their results were examined. Hospital data for all study periods were extracted together. Model hashes identify the evaluated model artifact but do not independently verify the timing of first cohort access, and the study was not prospectively registered. The primary strategy endpoint, alert budgets and comparator rules were specified after development review and before first access to the 2025 and 2026 hospital cohorts. Supplementary sensitivity, ablation and workflow analyses followed the primary evaluations and did not select a replacement for D18. Table S16 summarises the analysis sequence and lists artifact identifiers for reproducibility. Analyses used Python 3.11.1 and scikit-learn 1.7.2; the three-algorithm comparison additionally used XGBoost 3.0.5. ChatGPT (OpenAI) assisted code development and editing, and Codex (OpenAI) and Claude (Anthropic) assisted manuscript revision.

The environment recorded Python 3.11.1, numpy 2.2.6, pandas 2.2.3, scikit-learn 1.7.2, scipy 1.15.3 and joblib 1.5.2. Source files, data hashes, saved model and score hashes identify the computational artifacts. No patient-level data are included in this supplement. Supplementary Software (Code_and_Model.zip; version D18_code_model_package_20261015_v1) provides the frozen models, analysis code, dependencies and synthetic examples. README_EN.txt describes its use and reproduction scope. Portable commands start from prepared feature tables; complete reproduction requires the original frozen inputs. Ethics details, data-access conditions and the final-report snapshot date remain to be completed.

S10 Specimen audit and additional-analysis scope

We independently reconstructed the T0 specimen category from the frozen request trace and mapping dictionary. The check used only co-earliest requests and compared the reconstructed categories with those used for prediction. We also tabulated known and unknown outcomes by T0 specimen category in each cohort and in the selected alert sets. This describes where unknown outcomes occurred; it does not identify their causes. Whole-event specimen descriptions in Table 1 use all requests within 24 hours and are distinct from the T0 predictor (Supplementary Tables S14 and S17).

Further D18 analyses examined outcome windows, grouped predictor removal, pathogen coverage, fixed thresholds with repeat-alert suppression, and restricted history (Tables S18–S26). Their plans were recorded before the corresponding calculations. The complete D18 model was retained as the baseline for all supplementary analyses.

Tables S18–S26 report D18-specific results, retaining all three budgets and reporting unfavourable as well as favourable comparisons. First-layer H90 remains a simulated history rule, not a deployed hospital alert system.

S11 Outcome-window sensitivity

We used three outcome windows: the full 24-hour event, all requests tied at T0, and a single T0 request selected using the previously fixed request-ID hash. Selection was independent of outcomes. Eligibility, scores, H90 flags and the co-earliest T0 specimen predictor were unchanged. The single-request analysis did not redefine a MULTIPLE predictor as a single specimen. Positive-event labels, H90-positive patient coverage and first known-positive events were recalculated for each outcome window. Thus, additional coverage can increase when H90 coverage falls; it is not evidence of finding more true infections. No model was refitted for these analyses. MIMIC-IV used specimen chart time as the T0 proxy. Table S18 includes outcome denominators, alert composition, coverage and paired rule comparisons at every budget.

S12 Laboratory and grouped predictor ablations

Four models were refitted using the full development population: removal of all five laboratory inputs (13 predictors); replacement of their categories by five usable-result-at-T0 indicators (18 predictors); removal of prior_target_reported_90d and prior_crgn_reported_90d (16 predictors); and removal of icu_status and length_of_stay (16 predictors). Result availability does not indicate whether a test was ordered. Every model retained T0 specimen category. Removing the two microbiology inputs affected the second-layer prediction model only: first-layer H90, medication and procedure history, and the recorded history data were unchanged. No H90 value was set to zero. Each fit used five patient-grouped outer folds, four inner folds, final five-fold tuning and training-fold-only preprocessing. C was selected from 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10 by mean log loss; all final models selected 0.1. Tables S19–S21 report all budgets, both performance populations and paired differences. These targeted removals assess conditional predictive information, not causal effects, stepwise variable selection or model equivalence. No result was used to replace the focal model.

S13 Pathogen coverage

For each covered patient, CRE, CRPA and CRAB evidence was combined across the selected positive events. Mutually exclusive categories were CRE only, CRPA only, CRAB only and multiple target groups. Multiple-group evidence need not come from one culture. Overlapping group coverage was reported separately and cannot be summed to obtain distinct patient counts. H90-covered and additional patients were disjoint. These outcomes describe resistant-organism detection, not infection, treatment response or clinical benefit (Table S22).

S14 Fixed thresholds and repeat-alert suppression

Thresholds were frozen from the development H90-unflagged distribution, using nested out-of-fold D18 scores and ICU-plus-length-of-stay rankings. Targets of 1%, 2% and 5% define development workload, not calibrated clinical risk. The same score or ICU/LOS threshold and fixed boundary hash were then applied to each cohort, without selecting a fresh local quota. We simulated 0-, 1-, 3- and 7-day suppression in patient time order. Only an emitted extra alert reset the interval; a suppressed candidate did not prolong it. H90 alerts were unchanged, no suppressed slot was refilled, and future information and outcome labels were not used for selection. Patient-cluster resampling retained the fixed thresholds and recomputed workflow outcomes. Table S23 reports both strategies, all targets and intervals; Table S26 records the thresholds. Realised workloads differed, so these results do not compare strategies at equal cost or establish an optimal suppression interval.

S15 Observable history and early-period refitting

Existing pre-T0 contact-span metadata classified events as ≥90 days, <90 days or unresolved; no event in the supplied strata had unresolved span. Development events were separately divided into the first 90 calendar days and later events. Ranking, quotas and H90-positive patient baselines were recomputed within each stratum. A patient could enter different strata at different times, so patient counts across strata must not be added. A long contact span does not establish continuous observation or confirmed absence of resistance. No formal interaction analysis was conducted. For the early-period refit, events before 29 February 2024 were excluded from development, while legitimate pre-T0 history was retained. This left 19,962 events, including 19,422 known outcomes. Nested fitting and final tuning were repeated; the later cohorts were unchanged. The development comparison used only the 19,962 common retained events for both models. Tables S24–S26 report the stratified analyses, refit comparisons and saved-model identities.

S16 Development comparison and selection of the L2 algorithm

Three algorithms were compared on the same 18 predictors, development events, outcome labels, H90 flags and patient-fold assignments. The development cohort comprised 25,350 events from 15,280 patients, of which 24,501 known-outcome events from 14,916 patients were eligible for fitting, including 1488 positive events. It covered 1 December 2023 to 31 December 2024. No 2025, 2026 or MIMIC-IV data were used for this algorithm comparison.

Each algorithm used five outer and four inner patient-grouped folds with training-fold-only preprocessing. Hyperparameter grids were declared before the corresponding fits: four L2 configurations, eight random-forest configurations and twelve XGBoost configurations. The lowest mean inner-fold log loss selected hyperparameters within each algorithm; a separate five-fold search on the full development data selected hyperparameters for the final fit. There was no early stopping, outcome resampling, class reweighting or SMOTE. Random forest used 500 trees; XGBoost used a learning rate of 0.05, subsample and column-sampling fractions of 0.8, L2 regularisation of 1 and no L1 regularisation. Table S27 gives the grids and selected values.

All reported comparisons used nested out-of-fold predictions, including held-out predictions for unknown-outcome events in the alert-allocation pool. Final full-development fits were not used to calculate these performance estimates. The comparison retained the 1%, 2% and 5% budgets, with particular attention to additional cumulative positive-patient coverage beyond H90 at 2%. First known-positive capture, event-level alert composition, alerts per additional patient, discrimination, probability loss and calibration were also evaluated. One thousand paired patient-cluster bootstrap replicates held model scores fixed, recomputed allocations and refitted calibration diagnostic regressions. The resulting pointwise intervals were not adjusted for multiple comparisons and do not cover the entire process of selecting predictors and an algorithm family.

After reviewing the development comparison, the authors retained L2 because its model structure was transparent and facilitated audit and reproduction, while improvements in the core coverage endpoint were uncertain. At 2%, random forest reached 12 more patients than L2 (95% CI −6 to 24) and XGBoost reached 5 more (−7 to 18). This rationale did not imply that L2 had the highest discrimination or lowest probability loss. Tree models improved several other measures, including first known-positive capture. Intervals spanning zero do not establish equivalence or non-inferiority, and no margin for either was specified. Algorithm selection was completed before first access to validation cohorts, as described in Method S9.

The retained L2 parameter was C = 0.1 in every outer search and in the final development search. Its six model files and nested predictions matched the existing D18 artifacts; the final file SHA-256 was 8f27b6f6bf656fa760f532624da3e12f8a2dde8f2c09ec5d08474d5a80db9d09. The RF execution was changed from four threads to one because of a Windows thread-pool permission failure, with the same grid, tree count, seed and sample. This execution change did not use performance results. Tables S28–S31 report favourable and unfavourable comparisons. The tree models were not evaluated in the temporal or external cohorts in this comparison.

S17 Transparent specimen rules and global-allocation strata

Two additional rules used the fixed six-level T0 specimen domain. Group order was learned from the positive proportion among H90-unflagged known-outcome development events. Groups without known outcomes were placed last; exact tied proportions used the fixed domain order RESP, BLOOD, URINE, OTHER, MULTIPLE, MISSING. The combined rule ordered events by specimen group, then ICU priority, decreasing pre-T0 length of stay and the existing fixed event hash. The specimen-only rule used group order followed by the same fixed hash. No evaluation outcomes determined group order. Development evaluation used orders learned within the outer training folds; later cohorts used the full-development order (Table S32).

H90 alerts, candidate events and 1%, 2% and 5% global extra budgets were unchanged. Unknown outcomes consumed capacity. Tables S33 and S34 report all strategy budgets and summaries of the selected events by T0 specimen category. Strata were described after global allocation; budgets were not reassigned within each specimen group. Within-specimen AUROC and AP in this analysis use all known outcomes, including H90-marked events, and must not be labelled H90-unflagged performance. Patient counts can overlap across strata. The rules produce rankings, not individual probabilities, so probability calibration is not reported for them.

Paired patient-cluster draws were shared by all strategies within each cohort, with fixed scores and learned orders. Seeds were 2026101601 plus cohort index (development, 2025, 2026 and MIMIC-IV indexed 0–3). All 1000 clinical replicates were valid; missing-specimen performance was not estimable because that stratum was empty. These post-primary comparisons are exploratory and do not change D18 or the original primary-comparison intervals.

S18 Returning-patient candidates and H90 evidence audit

Candidate recovery identified 6647 returning events across all ages, including 6097 adult events: 2802 events from 1380 patients in 2025 and 3295 from 1647 patients in 2026. Table S35 records this screening stage and the H90 audit in the primary cohorts. Composite outcomes and full predictor/history snapshots were reconstructed for 6096 adult events; one event with a T0 mismatch was excluded. Methods S23 and Tables S46–S48 report the evaluated returning and expanded populations.

For the original hospital cohorts, H90 flags were reconstructed from saved qualifying report links using strictly pre-T0 availability, the 90-day window, and exclusion of all requests in the current event. All 757 flags in 2025 and 751 in 2026 were reproduced. The 1902 and 1605 history links had no observed window, availability or current-event violations and arose from earlier included events in the same year. Among 424 and 367 patients with a first known-positive event, none had an H90 flag or qualifying recorded resistant history at that event. The implementation did not force first capture to zero. These observations do not establish absence of resistance outside the retained evidence or a universal structural zero.

S19 Restricted respiratory-flora outcome hypothesis in MIMIC-IV

This endpoint-only sensitivity retained the original candidate events, D18 scores, H90 flags, history mappings and alert selections. An original unknown unit could be treated as hypothetically non-positive only when its actual specimen mapped to respiratory, it belonged to the flora-or-incomplete-workup branch, it contained normal commensal respiratory or oropharyngeal flora wording, no organism was named and no susceptibility drug was tested. Mixed or contaminated wording, cancellation or pending status, abbreviated or incomplete identification, and low-quantity hints excluded the unit. A source review found respiratory-flora wording attached to non-respiratory specimens; the explicit actual-respiratory-specimen requirement was therefore retained. This is an assumption, not clinical confirmation of a negative result.

The event remained positive if any unit was positive, became hypothetically non-positive only when every unit supplied sufficient original non-positive evidence or met this assumption, and otherwise remained unknown. Positive-patient baselines and first known-positive events were recalculated under the labels, while selections stayed fixed. The positive set was unchanged, so both coverage endpoints were invariant. The analysis does not harmonise carbapenem sets, source report-status fields, taxonomy, susceptibility-conflict handling or history mappings (Tables S36–S38). Patient bootstrap draws were shared across both label versions, using seed 2026101621; all 1000 replicates for the reported performance and clinical metrics were valid.

S20 Resistant pathogens and actual positive specimens

Pathogen evidence was linked through the same formal positive request in hospital data or micro_specimen_id in MIMIC-IV. Joint counts were not reconstructed from separate marginal tables. Susceptibility rows and repeated isolates were deduplicated within each pathogen–specimen cell at event and patient levels. For H90, only H90-alerted positive events contributed evidence. For D18 additional coverage, evidence came only from D18-selected positive events at 2%, after excluding patients covered by H90 on any positive event in the evaluation period. A patient or event can occur in more than one pathogen–specimen cell; sums need not equal distinct cohort totals (Table S39).

The actual positive specimen can differ from the co-earliest T0 specimen category, because later requests within the fixed 24-hour event may supply outcome evidence. Table S40 explicitly separates these concepts for additional coverage; Supplementary Data 1 includes the full cross-tabulations and raw specimen categories. No clinical chart adjudication of infection, colonisation or contamination was undertaken.

S21 Training restricted to H90-unflagged development events

A supplementary L2 model used only H90-unflagged development events with known outcomes: 22,826 events from 14,915 patients, including 805 positive events. The constant prior_crgn_reported_90d input was removed, leaving 17 raw inputs, including T0 specimen type and target-organism history. Eligibility was event-specific and did not exclude patients based on later H90 flags. The original five patient folds were retained; five outer/four inner folds and a final five-fold development search selected C from 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10 by mean held-fold log loss. Preprocessing was fitted within training folds. Outer models selected C values 1, 1, 1, 0.1 and 0.1; the final model selected C = 10. It has 49 encoded coefficients plus an intercept (Table S41).

Validation used the same H90-unflagged candidate events and original first-layer H90 rule as D18. Unknown outcomes were excluded from fitting and known-outcome performance, but retained in extra-alert allocation. Development scores were out of fold. No evaluation data were used for fitting or penalty selection. This restricted-training model is a post-primary sensitivity analysis and does not replace the 18-input D18. Tables S42 and S43 report performance and all alert budgets, including unfavourable differences. Seeds were 2026101640 plus cohort index 0–3; all 1000 reported replicates were valid. Intervals spanning zero do not establish equivalence or non-inferiority.

S22 Fixed stronger specimen-aware comparators

R1 and R2 were specified after the concentration of D18 alerts in respiratory or multiple-group T0 specimens (Tables S14 and S34) had been observed and were intended as deliberately strong simple comparators. Because their design was informed by validation findings, these comparisons do not provide a prospective test against an independently specified rule. These post-primary exploratory comparisons retained frozen D18 scores, original cohort membership, H90 and the 1%, 2% and 5% global quotas. S = 1 denoted RESP or MULTIPLE at T0, at equal priority; MULTIPLE need not contain a respiratory request. R1 ranked ICU descending, S descending, finite pre-T0 LOS descending (nonfinite values last), then the existing ascending event hash. R2 exchanged the ICU and S priorities. No rule fitting, grid search or outcome-based selection among these two forms was performed. Both forms and all budgets are reported; the earlier development-derived specimen combination is retained for context (Tables S44–S45).

R1 and R2 selected identical event sets in every reported cohort/budget: the ICU-and-S stratum exceeded each quota. Their identical performance therefore provides one effective selection contrast, not two independent replications. Unknown outcomes consumed quota, and specimen-stratum summaries followed global allocation without subgroup quotas. This analysis used 5000 paired patient-cluster draws, with seeds 2026101601–2026101604 for development, 2025, 2026 and MIMIC-IV. D18 development scores were nested out of fold; validation scores were unchanged final-model predictions. All reported interval rows had 5000 valid replicates. Original 1000-replicate primary intervals were not replaced. Tables report pointwise intervals without multiplicity adjustment.

S23 Returning-patient extension

The extension used saved reconstructed predictors, composite labels and pre-T0 report evidence, without a new complete source query or D18 refitting. Of 6097 adult candidates, 6096 were scored: 2802 in 2025 and 3294 in 2026. One 2026 event had a 32-second T0 disagreement and was excluded without a substituted score or outcome. All 1380 returning patients in 2025 and 787 in 2026 had appeared in the adult development cohort. Final-fit membership is given in the overlap CSV. The extension therefore evaluates a broader patient population that overlaps development.

Original and returning patients were disjoint within each evaluation year. Expanded evaluation concatenated their events before recomputing the global candidate pool, ceil(N × budget) quota, ranking, H90-positive-covered patients and earliest positive events. Separate returning-only analyses used their own scope and quotas; their counts cannot be added to original-cohort counts to infer expanded coverage. Current-event eligibility used age at T0; valid pre-T0 histories could precede age 18 or the evaluation year. H90 excluded current-event reports and required strictly prior availability within 90 days. The evidence checks found no recorded flag mismatch or timing/current-event violation. Expanded first H90 capture was 14 and 19, all from returners; the original zeros were not forced by code.

Score replay across 84,388 event rows gave a maximum absolute difference of 1.11 × 10−16; original cohort inputs were unchanged. Reproducing source extraction requires private reconstruction tables and report evidence that are absent from the aggregate archive. Bootstrap evaluation used 5000 paired patient-cluster draws within each year and population. Seeds for 2025/2026 were 2026101761/2026101762 (original), 2026101861/2026101862 (returning) and 2026101661/2026101662 (expanded). All reported interval rows had 5000 valid draws. Tables S46–S48 give flow, prediction performance and strategy outcomes. Supplementary Data 1 contains all budgets, membership allocations, overlap, case mix and history-evidence summaries.

S24 Identification bounds conditional on the respiratory-flora hypothesis

The original MIMIC-IV mapping (O) retained 78,219 unknown outcomes. The restricted hypothesis (F), defined in S19, locked 9721 otherwise unknown events as non-positive while leaving 68,498 unknown events. All 1181 observed positive events and all 310,977 candidates remained. These assumed labels were never reopened during bounding. Scores, selections and H90 flags stayed fixed; positive-patient H90 baselines and earliest positive events were recomputed for each completed label vector. Every comparison applied one shared completed label vector to both strategies, rather than assigning labels independently in their favour. ICU priority used its exact expected coverage.

For each mapping and 1%, 2% or 5% budget, the analysis reported sharp fixed-sample bounds for D18 minus ICU+LOS and D18 minus ICU priority, for cumulative additional coverage and first capture (Table S49). Bounds are identification regions under allowed labels, not confidence intervals. The restricted regions were nested within the original regions, and the original twelve bounds were reproduced in the execution audit. Uniform all-remaining-unknown-non-positive and all-remaining-unknown-positive scenarios are illustrative assignments and need not attain extrema. Table S50 reports their paired percentile intervals from 5000 patient-cluster draws, using seed 2026101621 and shared draws across mappings. The flora assumption does not confirm negative status, adjudicate infection, or harmonise all drug panels, taxonomy, report status and histories.

Additional sensitivity results

Across four predictor ablations, H90-unflagged AUROC ranged from 0.872 to 0.875 in 2025, 0.867 to 0.873 in 2026 and 0.730 to 0.743 in MIMIC-IV, versus 0.874, 0.871 and 0.746 for D18. At 2%, no ablation increased additional coverage with a paired interval excluding zero; removing microbiology-history inputs reduced 2026 coverage by 10 patients (95% CI 1–18 fewer). Training only on H90-unflagged development events also did not establish improved coverage: restricted-training-minus-D18 differences were 2 (−6 to 11), −7 (−15 to 1) and −9 (−17 to 1). These post-primary comparisons do not establish equivalence or replace D18 (Supplementary Fig. S1; Supplementary Tables S19–S21 and S41–S43).

Independent T0 reconstruction covered all 372,425 original events with no category mismatches, excluding later requests from predictor derivation. This verifies consistency with frozen traces, not historical source-field revisions. Using a single T0 request for the outcome gave 2% additional coverage of 59, 56 and 58 patients, with differences from ICU+LOS of 19 (95% CI 7–33), 37 (23–49) and 31 (17–44). Outcome-window changes also alter H90-covered patient baselines and do not measure additional infections (Supplementary Tables S17–S18).

History-stratified and early-period refitting results are in Supplementary Tables S24–S25. At 2%, ICU+LOS contrasts in the ≥90-day contact-span strata included zero in both hospital years; shorter-span contrasts were positive, without a formal interaction test. Excluding the first 90 development days did not establish a coverage difference in any validation cohort. Contact span does not ensure continuous observation.

Additional hospital results

None of the four predictor ablations or the H90-unflagged training model established improved additional coverage at 2%. Removing microbiology-history inputs reduced 2026 coverage by 10 patients (95% CI 1 to 18 fewer). Supplementary Figure S1 and Tables S19–S21 and S41–S43 give these exploratory comparisons; intervals spanning zero do not establish model equivalence.

D18 also alerted the first known-positive event of 90 patients in 2025 and 70 in 2026. Differences from ICU+LOS were 29 (95% CI 12 to 44) and 35 (19 to 49). H90 first capture was zero in both primary hospital cohorts, despite its cumulative coverage later in the period. The history audit reproduced these flags from valid pre-T0 evidence without forcing first capture to zero (Supplementary Tables S10 and S35).

Including returning patients expanded the 2025 cohort to 23,111 events from 14,374 patients and the 2026 cohort to 19,083 events from 11,637 patients. The extension evaluated 2802 returning events from 1380 patients in 2025 and 3294 from 1647 in 2026, with one further 2026 candidate excluded for the T0 mismatch. At 2%, D18 reached 65 additional patients versus 49 for ICU+LOS and 62 for R1/R2 in 2025; the corresponding 2026 counts were 54, 24 and 40. D18-minus-R1/R2 differences were 3 (95% CI −9 to 17) and 14 (2 to 26), while differences from ICU+LOS were 16 (−1 to 29) and 30 (18 to 45). Returning-only coverage was limited: D18 reached 5 and 3 additional patients, versus 6 and 5 for R1/R2. H90 first capture among returners was 14 and 19. These expanded cohorts included development participants (Supplementary Fig. S2b; Supplementary Tables S46–S48).

Additional MIMIC-IV transportability results

Among H90-unflagged known outcomes in MIMIC-IV, AUROC was 0.746 and average precision 0.020. At 2%, D18 generated 6176 extra alerts and reached 67 additional positive patients, increasing combined coverage from 26.56% to 37.54%. Burden was 92.18 alerts per additional patient, and known-outcome positive predictive value was 3.34% (121/3622). D18 exceeded ICU+LOS by 30 patients (95% CI 14 to 46). R1/R2 reached 45 patients and the development-derived specimen combination 24; D18-minus-R1/R2 was 22 (8 to 36). H90 captured 17 first known-positive events, and D18 added 89; the first-capture difference from ICU+LOS was 36 (17 to 55) (Tables 2 and 3; Fig. 4 and Supplementary Fig. S2; Supplementary Table S10).

Unknown outcomes affected 2554 of 6176 extra D18 alerts (41.35%) in MIMIC-IV. The respiratory-flora hypothesis assigned 9721 events as hypothetically non-positive, leaving 68,498 unknown events and reducing unknown extra alerts to 1978 (32.03%). Observed positive events and patient coverage were unchanged. At 2%, cumulative sharp bounds for D18 minus ICU+LOS narrowed from [−566, 1304] to [−458, 1041], and first-capture bounds from [−627, 1338] to [−514, 1074]. Both still permitted reversal of the observed difference. Assigning all remaining unknown outcomes positive also reversed first capture relative to ICU priority under both mappings (Supplementary Fig. S2c; Supplementary Tables S36–S38 and S49–S50).
